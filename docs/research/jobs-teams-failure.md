## Ten usable principles

### JTF-01 — Find exceptional contributors through work evidence

**Evidence:** JTF-B lines 6–9, 0:33–1:03, and lines 15–16, 1:49–2:00, report Jobs's emphasis on talented colleagues and small strong teams. This appears inside the sponsor preamble, separate from the 1985 interview.

**Application:** For hiring and team design, identify the work to be done, inspect candidate contributions and learning ability, and explain the evidence behind the proposed team. For agent teams, inspect capability against the actual task rather than merely adding roles.

**Limit:** Productivity ratios in this preamble are Jobs's reported estimates, not measured constants. “A player” must not become a factual label without criteria. More people are sometimes necessary for coverage, reliability, or specialized expertise.

### JTF-02 — Make dissent possible, and change decisions when facts warrant it

**Evidence:** JTF-C lines 450–459, 45:59–46:36, quotes Catmull describing factual disagreement sustained without yelling until Jobs changed his mind. Contrast lines 297–303, 30:05–30:43: executives became silent when Jobs entered, despite previously voicing concerns.

**Application:** State the strongest objection and the evidence that would reverse a recommendation. Preserve unresolved disagreement in decisions. Invite domain experts to correct assumptions.

**Limit:** Catmull's recollection describes one relationship, not proof that every employee could safely disagree. Combative behavior can suppress exactly the information needed to decide well.

### JTF-03 — Give specialists ownership while preserving product judgment

**Evidence:** JTF-A lines 443–449, 44:55–45:27, reports Jobs learning to give talented people room at Pixar. JTF-C lines 491–498, 50:12–50:51, describes Jobs handing the technical demo to Avie Tevanian and acknowledging engineering concerns.

**Application:** Name an owner for each decision, let the relevant specialist establish technical facts, and review the integrated customer experience. Distinguish product acceptance from personally deciding every implementation detail.

**Limit:** This coexists with the close interface reviews in JTF-A lines 618–620, 1:02:59–1:03:13. The defensible synthesis is selective involvement, not either total delegation or total control.

### JTF-04 — Build a durable team capable of successive products

**Evidence:** JTF-A lines 583–590, 59:20–1:00:06, reports Jobs describing talent, culture, and working together as the foundation for repeated products. JTF-C lines 58–64, 5:56–6:35, contrasts his stated wish for a permanent ensemble with NeXT turnover, then later retention.

**Application:** Architecture decisions should include maintainability, knowledge transfer, and the team's ability to deliver the next product. Hiring decisions should consider how people collaborate over time.

**Limit:** Mission statements do not establish healthy retention. The NeXT contrast means the skill must compare expressed values with observable behavior and outcomes.

### JTF-05 — Protect creative work from needless organizational distance

**Evidence:** JTF-B lines 155–161, 18:51–19:40, reports Jobs criticizing layers that separate decision-makers from contributors and drive talented people away. Lines 284–288, 35:00–35:32, discuss whether an established Apple would welcome unfamiliar new ideas.

**Application:** Ask who can make the decision, which approval step adds value, and how a promising idea can receive a small concrete trial. Keep contributors close to product evidence.

**Limit:** This does not prove all management or process is waste. Coordination, compliance, and quality controls can be necessary. Simplify a particular bottleneck with evidence.

### JTF-06 — Bound perfectionism by usability, time, cost, and manufacturability

**Evidence:** JTF-C lines 178–184, 18:02–18:37, describes revisions delaying delivery and making technology less novel. Lines 254–263, 25:44–26:37, links material/finish choices to production difficulty and returned units without a tracking system.

**Application:** For every proposed polish or engineering requirement, specify its user benefit, cost, delivery effect, and quality criterion. Define a smaller acceptable release and a way to learn from defects.

**Limit:** These specific NeXT manufacturing details remain secondary claims. They challenge “perfect everything” as doctrine; they do not establish that visual craft is worthless or that every deadline outranks quality.

### JTF-07 — Treat customer constraints and bad news as inputs

**Evidence:** JTF-C lines 209–215, 21:12–21:49, describes university advisers specifying a price constraint while receiving a logo brochure instead of a prototype. Lines 230–237, 23:20–24:03, contrasts an executive's warning with Jobs later experiencing the same product problems himself. Lines 194–196, 19:41–19:54, reports engineers concealing the real timeline.

**Application:** Record budget, actual user behavior, observed failures, and engineering uncertainty before recommending a feature. Maintain an honest estimate range and surface facts that undermine the preferred plan.

**Limit:** Do not repeat NeXT's quoted prices as comparable figures without configuration/date checks. The case supports respecting constraints, not treating every customer feature request as correct.

### JTF-08 — Persevere in the purpose while revising the mechanism

**Evidence:** JTF-A lines 518–521, 52:35–52:52, reports Catmull's view that creative work includes failures. JTF-C lines 374–388, 38:11–39:36, describes the software opportunity emerging from hardware difficulties; lines 413–420, 42:10–42:53, describes a practical pivot and improving financial results.

**Application:** Separate the enduring user problem from the current solution. Specify a test and stop/pivot criterion; preserve learning when a mechanism fails.

**Limit:** Later success does not show the original plan was right or that continued spending always pays. Exact financial results here are unverified secondary claims. Hindsight must not replace a present-tense test.

### JTF-09 — Negotiate around essential outcomes and the actual relationship

**Evidence:** JTF-A lines 571–577, 58:01–58:39, reports Gates contrasting Jobs's simple negotiating priorities with a more complicated earlier negotiation. JTF-C lines 330–334, 33:33–33:59, recounts an IBM presentation no-show and the reported lesson against overplaying leverage.

**Application:** Identify the few required outcomes, the other party's incentives, credible alternatives, and relationship commitments before recommending a deal. Separate desired leverage from verified leverage.

**Limit:** The two narrations give different details of the IBM collapse. Preserve both as reported accounts; do not invent a reconciled chronology. A simple priority list does not imply the resulting contract has only two terms.

### JTF-10 — Demonstrate the claim through a working experience

**Evidence:** JTF-C lines 487–504, 49:48–51:27, contrasts a prepared NeXT presentation with working software and a rival's presentation. JTF-B lines 113–120, 13:46–14:40, reports Jobs describing public communication as education and the product as the ultimate evidence.

**Application:** Feature proposals and presentations should show the user problem, the experience, and observable proof. Use a functioning demonstration or accurately labeled prototype where possible; explicitly identify simulated behavior.

**Limit:** One acquisition pitch does not prove presentation caused the outcome. Do not infer historical competitor inferiority from host rhetoric. A convincing demo still requires reliability, cost, and real-world validation.

## Primary-source checks

1. **Jobs's own interview and correspondence:** [Steve Jobs Archive, *Make Something Wonderful*](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), sections “Interview for In the Company of Giants” (1997), and the October 1, 1995 email to Andy Grove. Canonical claims are recorded under JOBS-TALENT and JOBS-REVISION in [principle cards](../../skills/thinking/jobs-musk-brain/references/principles.md). These checks corroborate selected principles, not every NeXT anecdote above.
2. **Failure in Jobs's retrospective voice:** [Stanford, prepared commencement address, June 12, 2005](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says). Jobs describes the Apple departure as painful, starting again, and eventually returning through Apple's acquisition of NeXT. This verifies the broad failure-and-return narrative behind JTF-08. It is retrospective testimony, not proof that those setbacks were necessary or that another founder should repeat them. His compressed account of Pixar should not substitute for its full institutional history.
3. **Contemporaneous agreement record:** [Microsoft, August 6, 1997 announcement](https://news.microsoft.com/source/1997/08/06/microsoft-and-apple-affirm-commitment-to-build-next-generation-software-for-macintosh/). Confirms continued Mac software development, $150 million in non-voting Apple stock, and additional terms involving browser distribution, patents, and Java compatibility. It corroborates the broad agreement behind JTF-09 while qualifying the podcast's compressed two-priority story. It does not independently verify Gates's account of negotiating speed or the five-year duration.

## Runtime architecture implications (design proposals)

- Store compact principles with source identity, speaker, date/context when known, precise locator, evidence type, caveat, and application. Keep transcript evidence separate from verified originals and our derived procedure.
- Each recommendation should connect **historical evidence → interpreted principle → current project evidence → proposed action/test**. Never jump directly from a famous name to a technical choice.
- When the founder's own conduct contradicts the stated principle, surface that contradiction. Do not train a timeless “Jobs personality” from every stage of his career.
- A user's present requirements and observed engineering facts determine whether a principle applies. Historical preference cannot establish which modern library, architecture, candidate, or market is best.
- The runtime cards must remain useful without the private attachments: include a short substantive paraphrase and caveat, with public sources where verified. Private line/timestamp locators preserve auditability, not a mandatory runtime dependency.
