# Evidence-backed principle cards

Read claims narrowly. **Supported claim** describes what the evidence establishes; **Application** is our proposed use in a new situation. Sources and their verification scope are in [sources.json](sources.json). J01–J08 identify supplied narrated transcripts, not Jobs recordings. Their locators refer to the first occurrence of text, with one-based lines and commentary timestamps. Cards are usable from these labeled paraphrases without access to the private attachments. Public verification of selected facts does not upgrade every claim in a transcript.

## JOBS-FOCUS — Choose commitments deliberately

**Supported claim:** Jobs reported cutting 70% of Apple's roadmap in 1997.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Speech to Apple Employees” (1997); inspected edited primary text. Supplied J01 lines 749–757, 1:16:33–1:17:19; J03 lines 787–795, 1:19:26–1:20:13 repeat the same underlying account.

**Application:** Name the outcome worth concentrating on. List what is deferred and the consequences of each cut. Prefer a smaller coherent product over competing commitments when the project evidence supports that choice.

**Limit:** The historical percentage is not a deletion quota. Removing recovery paths or essential capabilities can make a product harder to use.

## JOBS-EXPERIENCE — Reduce the user's learning burden

**Supported claim:** Jobs used telephone accessibility to contrast with specialist command learning.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Speech at the International Design Conference in Aspen” (1983); inspected edited primary text. Supplied J01 lines 330–348, 33:28–35:22; J03 lines 354–373, 35:33–37:29.

**Application:** Observe whether a new user can complete the core action without learning internal implementation terms. Review the actual interface and help together, without a developer narrating it.

**Limit:** Expert tasks can justify training. Familiarity is not proof of usability; test the relevant task and user.

## JOBS-DEMOS — Make the disputed behavior concrete

**Supported claim:** The Creative Selection commentary relays Kocienda's account of successive concrete demos, feedback, and refinement.

**Evidence:** J04 lines 208–230, 21:13–23:34; secondary narrator relaying a participant's memoir. No direct Jobs recording was verified for this account.

**Application:** State the uncertainty, build the smallest artifact exposing it, gather feedback, and show how the next iteration addresses it. For a presentation, distinguish a functioning demo from a concept or simulation.

**Limit:** A demo can conceal reliability or demand problems. It establishes only what was actually demonstrated. The historical review process is not an instruction to micromanage every task.

## JOBS-PRIORITY — Make an important quality observable

**Supported claim:** Apple's Safari launch emphasized speed. The supplied commentary describes speed as its internal priority.

**Evidence:** ASAFARI, [Apple Unveils Safari](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2003/01/07Apple-Unveils-Safari/), opening paragraphs and Jobs quotation, 2003-01-07: inspected company announcement. J04 lines 180–187, 18:25–19:08: mediated memoir account. The announcement corroborates the public emphasis, not the entire internal process.

**Application:** Give the current product slice a dominant outcome, a measurement method, and minimum standards for other required qualities.

**Limit:** Apple's comparative launch benchmarks were not independently reproduced. A performance preference does not justify failure of correctness, usability, or other actual requirements.

## JOBS-REVISION — Let better reasoning change the decision

**Supported claim:** Jobs reversed a position after Andy Grove's challenge in October 1995.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Email Exchange Between Steve, Intel CEO Andy Grove, and an Intel Engineer” (1995), October 1 response; inspected edited primary text. J01 lines 485–500, 49:34–51:11; J03 lines 508–525, 51:22–53:03.

**Application:** State what would overturn the recommendation. Revise it explicitly when that evidence arrives.

**Limit:** One reversal does not establish that Jobs always welcomed criticism. Preserve conflicting accounts rather than manufacturing a consistently ideal personality.

## JOBS-DISSENT — Keep challenge available to the decision maker

**Supported claim:** Jobs invited Avie Tevanian to continue challenging him in August 1997.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Email Exchange with Avie Tevanian” (1997), August 25; inspected edited primary text. J01 lines 743–747, 1:15:59–1:16:22; J03 lines 781–786, 1:18:50–1:19:21.

**Application:** Surface the strongest substantive objection, and decide through reasoning and evidence. Give specialists room to correct technical premises.

**Limit:** J08 lines 297–303, 30:05–30:43 report employees becoming silent around Jobs at NeXT. That secondary counterexample cautions against treating expressed openness as proof of actual information flow.

## JOBS-ALIGNMENT — Share the destination, adapt the route

**Supported claim:** Jobs described goal alignment with flexibility about methods in 2003.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Speech at Stanford's Graduate School of Business” (2003), management discussion; inspected edited primary text. J01 lines 994–1012, 1:41:17–1:42:59; J03 lines 1028–1045, 1:43:18–1:45:07.

**Application:** Keep a brief shared account of the user, desired result, constraints, and success evidence. Delegate implementation choices while reviewing the integrated result.

**Limit:** Shared conviction does not establish demand or feasibility. Update the goal when evidence changes its value, not just the implementation route.

## JOBS-TALENT — Evaluate relevant work

**Supported claim:** Jobs discussed assessing demonstrated results when hiring.

**Evidence:** SJA, [Make Something Wonderful](https://book.stevejobsarchive.com/), “Interview for In the Company of Giants” (1997), hiring discussion; inspected edited primary text. J07 lines 6–16, 0:33–2:00 contain later-interview talent material in a sponsor preamble; it is not established as part of the central 1985 interview.

**Application:** Define the work, inspect task-relevant contributions, and choose observable criteria for collaboration and learning. Identify the evidence missing before judging a candidate.

**Limit:** Productivity multipliers in the narrated preamble are reported estimates, not reliable constants. Avoid converting promotional language into factual labels about people.

## JOBS-LIMITS — Ambition must confront constraints and failure

**Supported claim:** The supplied NeXT commentary reports shipping delays from revisions, customer price constraints, and manufacturing difficulty. Jobs's commencement account acknowledges departure, restarting, and return.

**Evidence:** J08 lines 178–184, 18:02–18:37; 209–215, 21:12–21:49; 254–263, 25:44–26:37: secondary narration of Steve Jobs in Exile. STANFORD, [2005 prepared commencement address](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2005/06/youve-got-find-love-jobs-says), second story: inspected primary retrospective account corroborates only the broad failure-and-return narrative.

**Application:** Connect a proposed quality requirement to its user benefit, time/cost impact, and test. Define a stopping or pivot criterion rather than explaining all failure as necessary progress.

**Limit:** The detailed manufacturing anecdotes are not independently verified here. Later success neither validates every earlier choice nor proves persistence always pays.

## JOBS-MESSAGE — Communicate a concrete benefit

**Supported claim:** Apple's iPod launch paired a pocket-sized music proposition with automatic synchronization. The presentation commentary interprets benefit-led communication as central to Jobs's approach.

**Evidence:** AIPOD, [Apple Presents iPod](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/10/23Apple-Presents-iPod/), product description and Auto-Sync, 2001-10-23: inspected company announcement. J02 lines 90–113, 9:22–11:42: narrator/Gallo interpretation. J06 lines 229–248, 23:18–25:16 attribute the paper-ball lesson to Lee Clow teaching Jobs. Supporting narrated examples: J02 lines 203–218, 20:57–22:30, numerical context; J04 lines 191–196, 19:30–20:00, Kocienda-reported rehearsal.

**Application:** State what improves for the audience, give relevant proof, and demonstrate the behavior. Rehearse important demonstrations and verify any derived metric.

**Limit:** A memorable claim is not proof of demand or causality. Keep Lee Clow's lesson attributed to him. Fixed message counts or practice ratios are not universal requirements.

## MUSK-FUNDAMENTALS — Rebuild the reasoning from demonstrated premises

**Supported claim:** Musk describes reasoning from fundamentals instead of convention or analogy, while allowing the conclusion to match precedent.

**Evidence:** MHF, [The Henry Ford oral-history transcript](https://www.thehenryford.org/docs/thehenryfordlibraries/innovator-transcripts/transcript_musk_full-length5a30f6547bde445e8119d53fb454b300.pdf?sfvrsn=f4eeb20a_1), printed pages 20–21, 03:16:40–03:17:47; pages 42–43, 04:08:00–04:08:59; interview 2008-06-26. Inspected primary transcript.

**Application:** Separate observations from conventions and derive the proposal from explicit premises. Calculate a lower bound only when the inputs and relevant costs are available; show assumptions and units.

**Limit:** A claimed fundamental can be wrong. An analogy may generate a useful hypothesis. A material-cost floor is not automatically a feasible delivered cost.

## MUSK-SEQUENCE — Question and remove before optimizing automation

**Supported claim:** The interviewer team's Starbase account reports Musk's ordered process: question requirements, try deletion, simplify, accelerate, then automate.

**Evidence:** MEA, [Starbase Tour and Interview](https://everydayastronaut.com/starbase-tour-and-interview-with-elon-musk/), “Musk's Engineering Philosophy,” numbered list, published 2021-08-11. Inspected interviewer-team summary, not a word-for-word transcript verification.

**Application:** Trace requirements to their rationale and owners. Identify deletion candidates and their consequences before optimizing surviving work. Automate only when the task's value and behavior are understood.

**Limit:** Deletion heuristics do not authorize removing required controls. The account includes manufacturing-specific advice on in-process testing; transfer to software requires its own risk and defect evidence.

## MUSK-SYSTEM — Evaluate the whole result

**Supported claim:** The Starbase account reports Musk asking engineers to understand the system to avoid optimizing the wrong component.

**Evidence:** MEA, [Starbase Tour and Interview](https://everydayastronaut.com/starbase-tour-and-interview-with-elon-musk/), paragraph after the engineering-process list about chief engineers and mass allocation. Same underlying interview as MUSK-SEQUENCE; not independent corroboration.

**Application:** Inspect end-to-end behavior and the actual bottleneck. Compare total integration, maintenance, and operating costs when a local optimization moves complexity elsewhere.

**Limit:** This is a proposed software adaptation. The historical example supplies no latency, cost, or scaling measurement for the current project.

## MUSK-TEAMS — Match people to work and allow informative failure

**Supported claim:** Musk describes task-dependent hiring, positive working relationships, and room for unsuccessful experiments.

**Evidence:** MHF, [The Henry Ford oral-history transcript](https://www.thehenryford.org/docs/thehenryfordlibraries/innovator-transcripts/transcript_musk_full-length5a30f6547bde445e8119d53fb454b300.pdf?sfvrsn=f4eeb20a_1), printed pages 44–45, 04:09:38–04:10:45; pages 52–53, 04:17:45–04:18:28. Inspected primary transcript, 2008-06-26.

**Application:** Evaluate the capability the task needs and how the team works together. Make experiments bounded and inspect their results, including failures.

**Limit:** These are dated self-reports, not an audit of every company or period. An acceptable experiment still needs defined exposure and learning criteria.
